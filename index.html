The Pentagon on September 11

A look at how an alternative claim—that a missile struck the Pentagon rather than an aircraft—circulated online, and how to test that claim against documented evidence.

Background / 01

Why did the claim spread?

After the attacks, limited early footage, confusion during a fast-moving emergency, and distrust of institutions helped create space for competing explanations. Online posts sometimes framed unanswered questions as proof of a missile strike. A question, however, is not evidence for a particular answer.

This project presents the missile claim as a subject for critical analysis—not as an established fact. The responsible approach is to identify the claim, locate its original source, and compare it with independent evidence.

Investigation file / 02

Security footage: what does it show?

Some online arguments focus on the appearance of the Pentagon security-camera recordings. A few frames from a low-resolution camera cannot, by themselves, identify every object or establish the full sequence of events.

To evaluate a video, ask: Is it the original recording? Is it complete? Is the timestamp verified? Has it been edited? What do other independent records show?

A video fragment should be treated as one piece of evidence, not as a complete reconstruction of an event.

No fabricated footage, images, quotations, or purported leaked documents are presented on this page.

Investigation file / 03

Physical evidence and the aircraft record

The missile theory must be assessed against the physical evidence documented at the site and the broader record concerning American Airlines Flight 77. The 9/11 Commission's account identifies Flight 77 as the aircraft that struck the Pentagon.

  • Look for documented recovery and identification processes.
  • Distinguish verified records from anonymous online claims.
  • Check whether a claim accounts for the full body of evidence, rather than one selected detail.
Investigation file / 04

Eyewitness accounts

Witnesses can provide valuable information, but people observe events from different locations, under different conditions, and for very short periods. Memory and perception can also be imperfect during traumatic events.

Good historical analysis compares multiple accounts with physical records, timelines, and other independently verifiable sources. A single ambiguous description does not prove that a missile was involved.

Evidence review / 05

What does the historical record conclude?

The established finding is that American Airlines Flight 77 struck the Pentagon. The missile claim is not supported by the evidence presented in the major historical investigation.

Start with these sources rather than reposts or unattributed screenshots:

  • The 9/11 Commission Report (PDF) — the commission's full investigation.
  • U.S. National Archives: September 11 records — archival records and resources.
  • National Institute of Standards and Technology — a source for understanding technical investigations and evidence standards.
CONCLUSION: This fictional website explores how a conspiracy claim is framed and evaluated. It does not endorse the claim that a missile hit the Pentagon. The documented historical conclusion is that Flight 77 struck the building.

Classroom use: identify the claim, separate observation from interpretation, check primary sources, and explain what evidence would change your conclusion.
